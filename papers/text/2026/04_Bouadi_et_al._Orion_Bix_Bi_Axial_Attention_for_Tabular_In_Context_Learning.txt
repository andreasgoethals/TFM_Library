Abstract
Tabular data drive most real-world machine learning applications,
yet building general-purpose models for them remains difficult.
Mixed numeric and categorical fields, weak feature structure, and
limited labeled data make scaling and generalization challenging. To
this end, we introduce Orion-Bix, a tabular foundation model that
combines biaxial attention with meta-learned in-context reason-
ing for few-shot tabular learning. Its encoder alternates standard,
grouped, hierarchical, and relational attention, fusing their outputs
through multi-CLS summarization to capture both local and global
dependencies efficiently. A label-aware in-context learning (ICL)
head adapts on the fly and scales to large label spaces via hierarchi-
cal decision routing. Delivered as a scikit-learn–compatible founda-
tion model, it outperforms gradient-boosting baselines and remains
competitive with state-of-the-art tabular foundation models on
public benchmarks, showing that biaxial attention with episodic
meta-training enables robust, few-shot-ready tabular learning.

art for tabular prediction. Recently, tabular in-context learning (ICL)
has emerged, adapting the ICL paradigm—central to large language

TabPFN [2] pioneered this approach by meta-training a trans-
former on synthetic datasets generated via structural causal models.
Its encoder–decoder design lets test samples attend to training ex-
amples for zero-shot prediction, but alternating column- and row-
wise attentions make large training sets computationally costly. Tab-
DPT [4] achieves comparable performance using similarity-based
retrieval, though its diffusion process adds overhead. TabPFN-v2 [3]
extended row-based encoding to datasets exceeding 10,000 samples.
TabICL [5] further refined this with a table-native transformer com-
prising column embeddings, row interactions, and an ICL head. Its
SetTransformer-based column encoder and label-aware in-context
learner achieve state-of-the-art results, but its row encoder (tf_row)
applies a single homogeneous attention over all features, ignoring
local groupings, multi-scale interactions, and structured aggrega-
tion. This limitation is pronounced in high-dimensional, heteroge-
neous tables where features naturally form semantic groups (e.g.,
demographics, vitals, labs) and dependencies span multiple scales.
Additionally, TabICL treats synthetic tables as individual supervised
tasks rather than support/query episodes, producing an implicit
few-shot signal that may misalign with test-time objectives.
To address these limitations, we introduceOrion-Bix1, which en-
hances TabICL with two key improvements: (1) abiaxial row encoder
replacing tf_row with complementary attention modes—standard
cross-feature, grouped, hierarchical, and relational—aggregated via
multiple CLS tokens, explicitly modeling local groups, coarse-scale
interactions, and global patterns; and (2) a meta-learning training
regime that constructs support/query episodes from synthetic tables,
optionally using kNN-based support selection, aligning training
with few-shot test-time objectives. Orion-Bix preserves TabICL’s
strengths in column-wise SetTransformer embeddings and label-
aware ICL while addressing its row-level and training limitations.
2 Orion-Bix: Proposed Approach
Orion-Bix retains two core TabICL [5] components: the SetTrans-
former column encoder and the label-aware in-context learner,
while introducing two major changes: (1) a biaxial row encoder
that replaces the single-stagetf_row and provides structured multi-
scale feature reasoning, and (2) a meta-learning training regime that
constructs explicit support/query episodes from synthetic tables,
optionally using kNN-based support selection.

2.3 Biaxial Row Encoder: Multi-Scale Feature
Interactions
The column embeddings 𝐸 ∈ R𝐵×𝑛×𝑚′ ×𝑑 provide per-cell feature
representations. The row encoder must transform these into row-
level summaries suitable for in-context reasoning. TabICL’s row
encoder applies a single Transformer stack over the feature dimen-
sion, treating all columns as a flat sequence. This forces one homo-
geneous attention pattern to capture (i) local dependencies within
semantically related feature subsets, (ii) long-range cross-column
interactions, and (iii) global aggregation into a single row vector.
In high-dimensional, heterogeneous tables, this uniform treat-
ment introduces three limitations: (i) no explicit feature group-
ing, despite tables containing natural substructures (e.g., demo-
graphics, vitals, lab tests); (ii) weak multi-scale structure, since
local and global relationships must be modeled through the same at-
tention mode; and (iii) a representation bottleneck, as collapsing
a row into a single embedding mixes distinct informational modes.
To overcome this, Orion-Bix replaces the flat row encoder with
a biaxial attention module that applies multiple, specialized feature-
space attentions and aggregates their outputs through multi-CLS
tokens, enabling structured, multi-scale row representations.

2.4 In-Context Learner
The in-context learner mirrors TabICL:
• It receives row representations 𝑅 ∈ R𝐵×𝑛×𝑑row and support
labels 𝑦train ∈ { 0, . . . , 𝐶 − 1}𝐵×𝑛train .
• Support labels are one-hot encoded, linearly projected, and
added to support row embeddings.
• A Transformer with a split mask enforces: support rows
attend to support only; query rows attend to support but not
to each other.
• An MLP produces logits in R𝐶max, where 𝐶max is the native
maximum number of classes.
• For tasks with 𝐶 > 𝐶max, hierarchical classification trees
compose node-wise probabilities by the chain rule.
2.5 Meta-Training with Synthetic Episodic Data
TabICL trains on synthetic tables drawn from a configurable prior,
but each table is treated as a single supervised task. Few-shot struc-
ture is only partially encoded through attention masks, leading to (i)
an implicit support–query signal and (ii) uncontrolled support se-
lection, where updates may depend on redundant or uninformative
examples.

Orion-Bix adopts an explicit meta-learning formulation on top
of a similar synthetic prior. Synthetic tables are sampled over wide
ranges of feature counts, label spaces, and distributions, and an
EpisodeGenerator converts each table into many small support–
query episodes. Episodes are created either by random splits or by
kNN-based support selection that ensures both relevance and di-
versity. A MetaLearningDataset streams episodes in manageable
chunks, and a MetaLearningTrainer processes thousands per up-
date using micro-batching, gradient accumulation, mixed precision,
and optional distributed data parallelism (DDP).
This episodic approach provides four advantages: (i) alignment
with the test-time objective, since the model is always optimized
in the regime in which it is evaluated; (ii) richer task diversity,
as each synthetic table yields many distinct episodes; (iii) higher-
quality supports, with kNN selection reducing redundant updates;
and (iv) scalable, stable optimization enabled by micro-batching
and distributed training.
2.6 Inference Pipeline and Practical Interface
Preprocessing and Feature Engineering. Given a table𝑋, Orion-Bix
preprocessor automatically: (i) detects numerical and categorical
columns and converts all features to numeric form; (ii) imputes miss-
ing values for both types; (iii) applies an optional normalization
scheme (none, power, quantile, or robust); (iv) clips outliers beyond
a configurable 𝑧-score; and (v) generates alternative column orders
via feature shuffling (e.g., circular shifts, random, Latin patterns).
Ensemble of Transformed Views. The preprocessor produces mul-
tiple transformed views of the dataset, each defined by a normal-
ization choice and feature permutation. For each view, a support–
query split is created and passed through Orion-Bix to obtain logits
for the query rows. Across views, logits are class-aligned, aver-
aged to form an ensemble prediction, and optionally converted
to probabilities via temperature-scaled softmax. This multi-view
ensembling improves stability and robustness, particularly under
skewed distributions or strong feature-order sensitivity.
3 Experimental Evaluation
We evaluate Orion-Bix against TabICL and other baselines to high-
light the benefits of biaxial attention and meta-learning. Our exper-
iments focus on three aspects: (i) domain-specific performance on
datasets with natural feature structure, (ii) support set quality to
assess robustness, and (iii) few-shot learning curves across varying
support sizes. These analyses target the improvements motivating
Orion-Bix: structured multi-scale feature interactions and enhanced
few-shot adaptation.
3.1 Evaluation Setup
3.1.1 Datasets. We construct domain-specific evaluation suites by
grouping datasets from public benchmarks (e.g., TALENT [6] and
OpenML-CC18 [1]) according to application domain, allowing as-
sessment in contexts where feature structure and heterogeneity are
most relevant. The domains are: Medical Finance. These domains
exhibit the high-dimensional, structured, and multi-scale features
that Orion-Bix is designed to handle.
3.1.2 Evaluation Metrics. For each dataset, we report overall clas-
sification accuracy (ACC), class-weighted F1 to account for imbal-
ance, and mean rank across datasets within each domain based on
accuracy. All models use official train/test splits unless noted.

3.4 Support Set Quality Analysis
We study how support set selection affects few-shot performance by
comparing random vs. diverse (kNN-based) sampling at inference.
This tests whether Orion-Bix’s meta-learning, which constructs
diverse support/query episodes during pre-training, improves ro-
bustness to support quality.
Protocol. Each test dataset is split 80/20 into train/test sets. A
support set of size 𝑘 = 32 is drawn using: (i) Uniform sampling,
ensuring all classes are represented, and (ii) Diverse (kNN-based),
selecting examples close to test queries and maximally diverse.
Models are evaluated with standard ICL on the same support/query
splits, repeated across multiple seeds for statistical significance.
Motivation. This protocol measures inference-time robustness: di-
verse selection simulates a scenario where practitioners can actively
choose informative support examples, while random selection rep-
resents default, arbitrary support sampling.
Results. Figure 2 shows that: (1) Uniform sampling slightly out-
performs kNN-based selection (by 3–5 points), indicating few-shot
performance is not highly sensitive to support curation; (2) Orion-

4 Conclusion
In summary, Orion-Bix preserves the core strengths of TabICL
while adding a biaxial row encoder and an episodic meta-learning
schedule that directly targets few-shot in-context performance.
